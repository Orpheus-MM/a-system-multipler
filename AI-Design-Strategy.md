# AI Design Strategy

AI fluency grows from repeated closed loops, not prompt tricks:

**Frame -> delegate -> inspect -> challenge -> synthesize -> apply -> measure**

Senior-designer advantage: judgment. AI increases range and speed; you still own problem definition, taste, evidence, tradeoffs, and organizational influence.

## Do Today

1. Pick one active project. Write the user problem, business outcome, known evidence, constraints, biggest uncertainty, and decision due.
2. Run four independent AI passes: product strategist, researcher, design critic, and skeptic.
3. Synthesize what changed your mind, what you rejected, missing evidence, and next experiment.
4. Build one small prototype for the riskiest assumption, not the whole feature.
5. End the day with a five-minute log: `decision / evidence / outcome / lesson`.

## High-Leverage Skills

- `accessibility-code-review`: WCAG, keyboard, focus, and ARIA.
- `accessibility-auditor`: structured product audit.
- `comments`: durable uncertainties, findings, and follow-ups.
- `diagrams`: flows, states, and system relationships.
- `quality-review`: broad implementation quality.
- `aiexpertsuite-help`: platform usage and Expert discovery.
- `cu-getting-started`: autonomous sessions and fleets.
- `cu-workflow-authoring`: repeatable multi-agent pipelines.
- `cu-schedule-authoring`: unattended recurring work.

Potential custom design skills: PRD-to-design-brief, design-system compliance reviewer, Figma-to-LWC handoff auditor, research-plan critic, interview synthesis, prototype accessibility check, design-decision recorder, and weekly product-change digest.

Create a skill only after the same prompt succeeds repeatedly. A skill is a proven method, not a saved wish.

## Overnight Runs

Start read-only. Never let overnight automation invent requirements, make product decisions, or mutate shared work.

Good overnight jobs:

- Summarize relevant product and documentation changes with source links.
- Audit a prototype against its PRD, SLDS, accessibility, and missing states.
- Compare implementation against Figma and list measurable differences.
- Cluster research notes while preserving quotes and source references.
- Generate adversarial scenarios: permissions, empty states, failures, latency, localization, and mobile.
- Build a morning brief covering changes, findings, uncertainty, and recommended attention.

Use this pattern:

`Collect -> verify sources -> analyze in parallel -> synthesize -> rank by impact -> save report`

Require each report to separate evidence, inference, uncertainty, recommendation, and human judgment. Use `--require-human` for destructive work. Bind each scheduled run to a narrow agent/tool allowlist. Validate with `cu schedules run-now` before trusting night execution.

## Cadence

### Daily

- Challenge one assumption.
- Explore one prototype or interaction.
- Document one decision.
- Test one AI technique on real work.

### Weekly

- Monday: product and technology change brief.
- Tuesday: deep customer or domain learning.
- Wednesday: workflow automation improvement.
- Thursday: critique with design, engineering, PM, and accessibility lenses.
- Friday: decision review and learning synthesis.

### Monthly

- Delete weak prompts and obsolete automations.
- Promote one reliable workflow into a reusable skill.
- Teach one method to the team.
- Compare intended outcomes against shipped behavior.
- Ask your manager where senior-level leverage remains weak.

## Senior Designer Growth

Senior is not better screens. Senior evidence:

- Turns ambiguity into clear decisions.
- Frames the right problem before designing a solution.
- Connects customer value, business outcome, and technical reality.
- Understands system, data, permission, failure, and lifecycle implications.
- Makes quality repeatable through guidelines and tools.
- Raises team judgment, not only personal output.
- Communicates tradeoffs clearly.
- Measures shipped outcomes and changes position when evidence disagrees.

Build evidence through problem briefs, assumption maps, system diagrams, research plans, decision logs, prototypes, accessibility audits, outcome reviews, reusable skills, and teaching notes.

## Learn Deeply

Avoid passive AI summaries. Use retrieval practice:

1. Ask AI to explain a concept.
2. Close the answer.
3. Explain it from memory.
4. Ask AI to attack your explanation.
5. Apply the concept to the current product.
6. Teach it to another person.
7. Revisit it after one week.

For every product area, model:

`Users -> jobs -> journey -> objects/data -> permissions -> states -> failures -> metrics -> business model -> architecture -> team ownership`

If you cannot explain each layer without AI, understanding remains rented.

## Product Updates

Create a focused watchlist: Salesforce platform and SLDS changes, LWC/base-component updates, accessibility standards, Agentforce patterns, AI Expert Suite releases, competitors relevant to current work, and internal strategy, research, incidents, and customer feedback.

For each update ask:

- What changed?
- Why now?
- Who benefits?
- What behavior becomes possible?
- What existing design assumption becomes invalid?
- What should the team test?

## Design Intelligence Loop

- Morning: retrieve evidence and product changes.
- Midday: run parallel framing and critique.
- Afternoon: prototype the riskiest assumption.
- Evening: record the decision and learning.
- Overnight: run a read-only audit and prepare a morning brief.
- Friday: inspect outcomes and improve the workflow.

Goal: not more AI usage. Better judgment, faster learning, stronger decisions, and reusable team capability.
